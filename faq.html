Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about joining Pack 269.

Between school, sports, and screens, finding meaningful time to connect as a family can be a challenge. Cub Scouts isn’t just another extracurricular activity where you drop your kids off—it’s an adventure you get to experience with them. Here is why families love being part of Pack 269:

Real Outdoor Adventures: We trade screens for hiking trails and campfires. From exploring local parks to weekend family camping trips, we give kids the chance to connect with nature and learn practical outdoor skills

Character in Action: We focus on building resilience, teamwork, and confidence. Through hands-on projects, community service, and earning badges, kids learn what it means to be a good citizen and a reliable friend.

A Built-in Community: It’s not just about the kids making friends—it’s a great way for parents to connect, too. You’ll be joining a supportive, local network of families who value community and shared experiences

Fun with a Purpose: From the Pinewood Derby to learning how to safely build a campfire, everything we do is designed to be incredibly fun while quietly teaching lifelong skills.

Cub Scouts typically meets once a month for den meetings, plus a monthly Pack meeting. Most meetings run about an hour. There are also optional events throughout the year like camping trips, service projects, and special activities. We understand families are busy, and participation is flexible!

Parental involvement is encouraged and makes Scouting a richer experience for everyone. At minimum, we ask that a parent or guardian accompany their scout to meetings.

Cub Scouts is a family program, and our Pack runs best when everyone pitches in. Every family plays a role in making the experience fun, organized, and meaningful for the Scouts. Simply put, the Pack cannot operate without volunteers.

We ask parents to support Den Leaders and Pack Leaders by volunteering when opportunities arise. We understand that schedules are busy and it is not always possible to help every time, and that is completely okay. No one is expected to volunteer at every event.

There are many different ways to get involved throughout the year. Some roles are quick and easy, such as helping set up or clean up at a Pack meeting. Other opportunities involve a bigger commitment, such as helping run an event, serving as a Den Leader, or joining the Pack Committee.

There are two types of fees: registration fees and Pack dues. Scouting America and Golden Gate Area Council registration fees are paid at the time of registration and support the Scouting program at the national, council, and district levels.

Pack dues are collected at the beginning of each Scouting year and cover the operating costs of our Pack, including each Scout's awards and patches, Den activities, volunteer registration, and expenses for official Pack events.

Note that our fall and spring campouts do not have additional fees but some optional activities (such as indoor overnights at museum) do carry activity fees.

We strive to keep costs as low as possible while delivering a fun, engaging, and well-rounded Cub Scout experience.
